The call
How to read this page
The callThe one call each tab is scored on, boxed where it stands: the combined call on the 30-min tab, the opening call on the 5-min tab (the pre-market call before the open).
Combined callEvery forecaster mixed by its track record, the 30-minute call since 10/06; What’s in it lists its voices and their say.
Usual oddsHow the average went at this time of day on recent sessions, the time-of-day odds; tap their row in What’s in it for each part of the day.
Base-rate guessAlways picking the usual odds’ favourite: the yardstick a read is judged against on the scoreboard, right often on its own because most half hours are flat.
JEV aloneJEV’s raw answer, for comparison, not a call. Corrected JEV, one of the combined call’s voices, is JEV with its usual errors taken out.
Direction lineIf price moves past the flat band, which way: its own mix of four forecasters and Claude. It never calls flat. Its score is plus when the average moves its way past the band and minus against; +10 or more is right.
ClaudeClaude’s own read of the same half hour, not in the call, scored after the close against its starting odds, the odds it was given from past days.
Opening callEvery 5 minutes through the first hour, on the average price over the next 10 minutes: JEV alone, no mix.
Pre-market callRead through the night from S&P futures, bond futures, bitcoin futures and the morning’s reports, on the first half hour’s average: JEV alone, checked 10 and 30 minutes after the settled open, four minutes after the bell.
SquaresOne key everywhere: filled when right, outlined when it stayed flat or was passed, rust when wrong, dashed while open; the oldest on the left.
ptsPoints of the S&P 500 index: 7,817 to 7,818 is one.
TimesEvery time is in this phone’s own time zone, named in the header.

A call is graded on the average price over its window: where each minute closed against the price at the read, averaged, in index points, and set against a flat band (the ± figure) narrower than an end price would get, because an average swings less than any one price. An unsure call is passed, never counted as a miss. The dashed questions are shadow questions, asked and logged, never graded and never weighted. A call is a forecast, not a trade signal.
