The three levels

Walls: the bar is the wall's share of all the gamma on the board, full at 30%. On the chart a wall is a thin line that says only where it sits; the shading around it is how many contracts rest at that price, darkest where the most do. Most contracts is a head count of calls and puts at one strike.

Reading the board
The three levels
Call wall

The nearest call-heavy pile of options above price, in this week's contracts. The bar is its share of all the gamma on the board, built from last night's open positions. Gamma measures how sharply an option responds when the stock moves.

A wall shows where positions sit. It is not a level price is known to react to. The label only ever means “above price”, so once price passes it the card greys it out, and the next scan moves the name to the next pile up. A wall marked heaviest carries more gamma than any other pile on the board.

Put wall

The same thing below price, for put-heavy piles. A call-heavy pile below price is not a put wall and does not show on the card.

Most contracts

The strike near price with the most option contracts on it, calls and puts counted together: last night's open positions plus today's trades. “Near” means within about one and a half typical days' moves of price. It is the amber line on the chart.

The textbook says dealer hedging holds price close to a strike like this. That depends on who owns the options, which the station can't see, and SNDK's record hasn't shown price settling at it, late in the day or on expiry Fridays.

The walls and this strike measure different things. The walls are gamma piles sorted by distance. This is a head count of contracts.

Why it can look light

This strike leads on contracts. The bars measure gamma from last night's positions, with calls and puts netted against each other and today's trades left out, so a strike where lots of calls and puts traded today can lead the count and barely register on the bars.

A location, not a forecast. These show where the options are, not where price is going. Whether SNDK's price tends to move toward any of them hasn't been established, so no number is given.
