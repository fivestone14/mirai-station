How to read this page
Today’s callThe combined forecast: every forecaster mixed by its track record (since 10/06). This is the call today’s calls are scored on; the voices control lists what went into it.
Usual oddsWhat SPX did this hour on recent days; the bar to beat. Tap it for each part of the day.
JEV aloneJEV’s own forecast, one of the voices mixed into the call; not scored as the call.
PointA point is one step of the S&P 500 index, e.g. 7,817 to 7,818.
Today’s callsOne square per 30-minute call today, oldest on the left: filled when right, outlined when not, dashed while open. Tap for the reads, which carry each call’s numbers.
SituationEach row is one of the facts JEV reads, its figure in index points; tap for the sentences JEV answers questions about, word for word.
RecordRight = called direction matched. The usual-odds guess always says what this time of day usually does; it is right often on its own because most half hours are flat. Score vs guess = how much better (+) or worse (−) a forecast’s odds are than that guess, with confident misses costing most. Too early to judge until 300 reads; tap for the learning loop.
5-min callThe opening hour’s call, made every 5 minutes on the average price over the next 10. Last 5 min = how far price moved since the previous read, and whether that was quiet, normal or busy for the time of day.

JEV answers small questions about labelled facts, one probability per option. A probability here is not a forecast of where price goes. The sums are forecasts, graded by the bars 30 and 60 minutes later; the dashed questions are shadow questions, asked and logged, never graded and never weighted. A call is a forecast, not a trade signal, graded on the average price over its window: where each minute closed against the price at the read, averaged, in index points, and set against a flat band (the ± figure) narrower than an end price would get, because an average swings less than any one price. Its direction decides right or wrong; its size at the end price, where price stood highest and lowest (“min 3” is the window’s third minute) and any sharp move are on the reads page, and an unsure call is passed, never counted as a miss. For now each call also keeps the old grade, on the end price alone, and a call the average could not grade stands on that, said so. The call is JEV’s answer to that question itself: where the average will sit, up, flat or down within the ± figure, with no unsure; the old question on the end price is still asked and graded beside it. Through the first hour the opening lane reads every 5 minutes and its call, on the average price over the next 10 minutes, leads the page until its last call closes; then the 30-minute call leads again. Each call looks ahead a fixed number of minutes, so a new call can start before the last one is graded: today’s calls show each as a square. Before the open, the pre-market lane reads S&P futures, 10-year bond futures, bitcoin futures and the morning’s reports through the night and makes one call about the first half hour: the average price over the 30 minutes after the first settled price, four minutes after the open. It is a forecast too, graded by the bars. Every time here is in this phone's own time zone.
