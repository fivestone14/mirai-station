Faster, steady or slower
What it means

Faster means options at that one strike are changing hands more quickly now than earlier today. Slower, less quickly. Steady, about the same.

A strike is one of the prices options trade at.

What it measures

Contracts changing hands at that one strike. Not the price of the stock, not where the price is heading, and not the number beside it.

It sets the latest stretch of the session against the stretch before it. Both are from today.

Strike 1,530, 16 September

Earlier that afternoon about 26 contracts a minute changed hands there. Over the last stretch, 36 — 1.4 times as much, so it read faster.

When nothing is marked

Early in a session there is not enough of the day to compare yet, so no strike is marked. On 16 September the first marks appeared at 10:03.

A strike where very little traded is left unmarked too — too little changing hands to tell a real change from noise.

A different measure, and the only one here that reaches back a day: everything traded at the strike today against what was already sitting there at last night’s close.

Busier, not going anywhere. How fast a strike trades says nothing about which way the price moves.
